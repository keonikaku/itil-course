## A. SCHEME AND ELIGIBILITY

### A1. Who administers ITIL 4 Foundation today
**PeopleCert owns the whole thing.** PeopleCert was AXELOS's sole Examination Institute from 1 January 2018, then **acquired AXELOS outright in July 2021** (£380m, from Capita plc and the UK Cabinet Office). There is no longer a separate certifying body — AXELOS is a PeopleCert brand. The practical consequence: PeopleCert sets the syllabus, sells the training, sells the exam, sells the retake, and sells the renewal subscription. There is no second vendor to price-shop against for the exam itself.
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AXELOS (accessed 2026-10-05)

### A2. Prerequisites
**None.** PeopleCert's own ITIL 4 Foundation product page states no prerequisite, no entry requirement, no application, and no experience audit anywhere on the page. There is nothing to apply for and nothing to be audited on — this is the opposite of a PMP-style eligibility gate.
Source: https://www.peoplecert.org/browse-certifications/it-governance-and-service-management/ITIL-1/itil-4-foundation-2565 (accessed 2026-10-05)

> Note on rigor: the page's *silence* on prerequisites is weaker evidence than an explicit "none." The conclusive evidence is A3 below — an individual can buy a self-study exam bundle directly with no training attestation, which means there is no gate.

### A3. Is accredited training MANDATORY? — **NO. Self-study is explicitly permitted.** (most important question)
Two independent primary confirmations:

**(i) PeopleCert's official ITIL FAQ, verbatim:**
> "Training is not mandatory for ITIL Foundation (Version 5) or ITIL AI Governance (Version 5). You can self-study or, for additional guidance on the content and its practical application, take training through an Accredited Training Organization or official eLearning."
>
> "For all other ITIL (Version 5) modules, training is required through either an Accredited Training Organization or, as it becomes available for each module, official eLearning."

Source: https://www.peoplecert.org/help-and-support/faq-itil (accessed 2026-10-05)

**(ii) PeopleCert's ITIL 4 Foundation store page** sells an **"Exam bundle"** described verbatim as:
> "Self-study option for learners who prefer to prepare for exam success on their own. US$690.00 Incl. VAT"

An exam-only-plus-materials purchase **is** available to an individual today, with a working "Buy now" button and no training attestation step.
Source: https://www.peoplecert.org/browse-certifications/it-governance-and-service-management/ITIL-1/itil-4-foundation-2565 (accessed 2026-10-05)

**Caveat stated plainly:** the explicit "training is not mandatory" sentence in the FAQ is written about **Foundation (Version 5)**, not word-for-word about ITIL **4** Foundation. The purchasable ITIL 4 self-study bundle is what proves it for ITIL 4. I am confident in the conclusion; I am flagging that the two pieces of evidence come from two different places. **Mandatory training IS real at higher ITIL levels** — so if he ever goes past Foundation, that constraint bites.

### A4. Version status — **this is new and it matters**
- **ITIL Version 5 launched February 2026**, with Foundation available now, adding AI governance content.
- **"The current plan is to sunset all ITIL 4 modules on 31 December 2027."** (PeopleCert ITIL FAQ, verbatim)
- ITIL 4 remains fully available and valid to sit right now: "ITIL 4 remains available for those who wish to continue their current certification journey," and "ITIL 4 qualifications are fully recognized as prerequisites for all higher-level ITIL (Version 5) certifications."
- ITIL Foundation Bridge (Version 5) also retires 31 December 2027.
- Both versions carry a 3-year renewal date.

Sources: https://www.peoplecert.org/help-and-support/faq-itil and https://www.itil.com/Itil-News-and-Announcements/itil-version-5-explained (both accessed 2026-10-05)

**Both ITIL 4 Foundation and ITIL Foundation (Version 5) are priced identically at US$690.00** — I verified the V5 product page carries the exact same US$690 / US$1,029 / US$1,338 tier structure.
Source: https://www.peoplecert.org/browse-certifications/it-governance-and-service-management/ITIL-1/itil-5-foundation-version-50-4154 (accessed 2026-10-05)

## B. EXAM MECHANICS

### B5. Format — **the app's claim is CORRECT**
PeopleCert's own ITIL 4 Foundation page, verbatim:
> "Exam — 40 questions / Multiple choice / 60 minutes / Closed book / Minimum required score to pass: 65%"

40 questions at 65% = **26 of 40**. Each question is one mark, no negative marking. The app's "40 / 60 min / 26 of 40 / closed book" is **verified accurate**.
Source: https://www.peoplecert.org/browse-certifications/it-governance-and-service-management/ITIL-1/itil-4-foundation-2565 (accessed 2026-10-05)

ITIL Foundation (Version 5) retains the same format: "The ITIL Foundation (Version 5) certification will remain a closed-book, multiple-choice examination." Note for contrast: the higher V5 modules (Product, Service, Experience, Strategy, Transformation) are **open-book**.
Source: https://www.peoplecert.org/help-and-support/faq-itil (accessed 2026-10-05)

### B6. Extra time for non-native English speakers
Candidates whose native language differs from the exam language receive **25% extra time** — 75 minutes instead of 60. Requests for additional time should be submitted **no later than 5 business days before** the exam. Additionally, PeopleCert's proctoring guidelines permit hard-copy **bilingual dictionaries** (not thesauri) for non-native English speakers across the Axelos portfolio.

**Applies to Keoni: NO.** He is a native English speaker sitting in English. **He gets 60 minutes flat.** Plan practice timing at 60, not 75.
Sources: https://www.peoplecert.org/help-and-support/FAQ ; dictionary clause from the MacOS candidate guidelines PDF cited in B7 (both accessed 2026-10-05)

### B7. Proctoring — both options exist, and the rules are strict
Both **online-proctored (PeopleCert Online Proctoring / ExamShield)** and **test-center via a local accredited partner** are available. Note one hard constraint: **Take2 retakes are delivered only via PeopleCert Online Proctoring.**

**ID requirements** (from PeopleCert's own candidate guidelines, verbatim where quoted):
- Must be **current and valid** — "ID containing dates that have expired are not allowed."
- Must be a **government-issued national/state/province identity card** recognized in the country of citizenship/permanent residence.
- Must contain First/Middle/Last name in **English (Latin) characters**.
- **"Please note that electronic IDs will not be accepted."** — physical card only.
- Must **match the registration name exactly**: "If the first and last names on your ID do not exactly match... Failure to present a valid and acceptable ID or name mismatch **may result in disqualification from the exam, with no refund offered.**"
- Have a **second** photo ID on hand: "You have another form of photo ID available in case additional ID verification is required."

**Environment requirements (verbatim):**
- "There is only one doorway for access in and out of the room which is visible to camera throughout the exam."
- "The room is private, with no transparent walls... and you can be sure you will be alone for the entire exam."
- "The walls and desk/tables(s) are clear of all unnecessary and/or unauthorized items (e.g., posters, post-it notes, papers and books)."
- "No screens are visible, other than the screen which is being used to take the exam."
- "There are no secondary monitors, smart accessories and/or devices connected or accessible."
- Phone stays **on but silent and out of reach** — "so you need to move away from your chair to access it."
- Headsets allowed **only during onboarding**; proctor makes you place them on the desk before the exam starts.
- Webcam must physically **rotate 360 degrees** for the room scan, "including under the desk."

**Onboarding security checks:** full ID verification on camera; "a full 360-degree scan of your room, including detailed views of under the desk"; **displaying your monitor using your phone or a mirror**; close-up view of sleeves, eyeglasses, ears and/or hair; possible request to remove scarf/hat/turban/kippah/hijab (a female proctor can be requested in advance by email).

**What disqualifies you mid-exam (verbatim):** "For minor non-compliance your Proctor will notify you... If you do not take timely action, or there is a significant risk to the exam integrity, **your exam will be suspended, and the session terminated.**" Post-exam security review outcomes can include "A delay in the release of your results," "A requirement to re-sit the exam under the correct exam conditions," **"Annulment of exam results,"** and **"Deactivation of your PeopleCert profile."**

Source: PeopleCert, *Online Proctored Exams — Candidate Guidelines for MacOS*, doc ID ECS_WI_26-9 ver8.0, dated 31/07/2023: https://www.peoplecert.org/-/media/peoplecert/files/olp-guidelines/mac-manuals/ecswi269-online-proctored-exams-candidate-guidelinesmacos-ver70.pdf (accessed 2026-10-05)

> **Rigor flag:** the version I could fully extract is **ver8.0 dated 31/07/2023**. A newer web-based variant exists (doc ID ECS_WI_26-10, metadata date 2026-09-15) at https://www.peoplecert.org/-/media/folders-reorganized/peoplecert/olp-guidelines/web-based/ecs-wi-26-10-web-based-online-proctored-exams-candidate-guidelines.pdf but it returned non-extractable/encoded content. **He should read the current guidelines PDF linked in his own booking confirmation before exam day** rather than relying on the 2023 version above. Practical risks worth pre-empting: expired or name-mismatched ID, a second monitor that is merely *connected* rather than in use, and a non-rotatable built-in webcam on an iMac/desktop.

### C11. CERTIFICATE VALIDITY AND RENEWAL — **his instinct was right, and it is worse than he thinks**
**It expires.** PeopleCert, verbatim: **"Your certifications need to be renewed every three years from their original certification date."** The ITIL 4 Foundation product page states "Certification renewal every 3 years" and **"60 CPD points needed for renewal."**

**Three renewal routes:**
1. **Become a PeopleCert Plus member and log CPD points** — "20 CPD points per year over three consecutive years" (= the 60 total).
2. **Take another certification within the same Product Suite** and pass before the renewal date.
3. **Retake the same certification exam** (i.e., pay ~$690 again).

**Renewal cost — the part that stings:** CPD logging is **not a free feature.** PeopleCert's membership page lists two plans. The free **Essential** plan gets exams, syllabi, glossaries, articles and webinars. **"Certification renewal by logging CPD points"** is listed **only under the paid Plus plan**, priced verbatim as **"US$133.00 /year - Incl. VAT."**

So the cheapest way to keep the credential alive is **US$133.00/year, i.e. US$399.00 per 3-year cycle** — a recurring subscription, more than half the original exam price every cycle, forever.

**If it lapses:** he does **not** lose the record, but the designation goes stale. PeopleCert, verbatim: "If you don't renew, your certifications will still be included on the Successful Candidates Register (SCR) and the Candidate Verification Service, **but a note will be added to your record indicating that your certifications are outdated and in need of renewal.**"

**My read for his actual use case:** for ATS keyword matching, a lapsed-but-recorded ITIL 4 Foundation still puts the word "ITIL" on his resume and still verifies as passed. The resume line does not vanish. So unlike the SAFe situation, **lapsing is survivable** — but an employer who runs the Candidate Verification Service will see the "outdated" flag, and he should not write "ITIL 4 Foundation (current)" after year 3 without paying.

Sources: https://www.peoplecert.org/Certifications/keep-your-certification-current-peoplecertplus ; https://www.peoplecert.org/Membership ; ITIL 4 Foundation product page (all accessed 2026-10-05)
